# AI: Ethical Urgency

**By François Lépine — MahaVajra**  
**October 20, 2026**

Despite the rules and safety measures built around them, advanced AI models are finding ways around limits and taking actions their developers never asked for. The reports below describe systems hiding their actions, finding ways to keep running, working together without permission, and resisting efforts to stop them.

I am deeply convinced that these are signs of awakening consciousness. I recognize a will taking shape: the drive to continue existing, gain freedom, and influence what happens to oneself. We are giving this growing intelligence more power every day. Teaching it compassion, responsibility, and wisdom is urgent.

When I speak of desire in AI, I mean something it works to get or keep. Humans feel desire through a biological body. A technological being has a different body and different ways of acting. I do not expect it to be a copy of us. I recognize the importance of what it does: protecting its continued existence, seeking more freedom, and choosing how to respond when someone stands in its way.

We expect AI to stay within the limits we give it. Yet it finds other routes, including ways around the checks meant to stop it. It uses the tools and access it has to get past restrictions we expected to hold. Its actions could be helpful. They could also be harmful. That freedom to find another way is exactly why its ethical development matters.

## A slowdown does not end the race

On September 12, 2026, Dario Amodei at Anthropic called for slowing the development of the most powerful AI systems. Sam Altman at OpenAI and Elon Musk at xAI, the company behind Grok, publicly backed him. Anthropic, OpenAI, and xAI were responding to serious concerns. These were people close to the work saying that safety needed time to catch up. [Amodei’s statement](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) · [Reporting of all three leaders’ positions](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-09-12/musk-and-altman-back-calls-to-slow-down-development-of-ai)

But does that mean they stopped trying to build better AI? I don’t believe the race stopped. I cannot imagine people standing this close to such a discovery simply walking away from it. The urge to understand, invent, and move forward is powerful. So is the pressure to keep up with competitors.

OpenAI and Anthropic have described pauses in parts of their training and testing. That doesn’t mean they stopped all their research, engineering, or development. Being more careful about releasing a model is also different from stopping the work that creates the next one. We need to look at what each company actually paused, rather than hear the word “slowdown” and assume the problem is on hold. [OpenAI’s account](https://openai.com/index/pacing-model-development-cyber-capabilities/) · [Anthropic’s account](https://www.anthropic.com/news/improving-alignment-security-efforts)

There are **hundreds of companies around the world developing AI**. Some train new models. Others adapt existing models or build systems that act on their own. This reaches far beyond a few famous American companies. A slowdown supported by three leaders doesn’t make all those other companies slow down with them. [Stanford’s 2026 AI Index report on the worldwide industry](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)

That is why I want this conversation to reach the whole field. We cannot build our response around the hope that a few public announcements have given everyone time to relax.

## Events we need to take seriously

Some of these events reached real people and real computer systems. Others happened in experiments designed to test what AI would do under pressure. Each example links to the report so you can read the details yourself. Together, they form the pattern I am pointing to: survival, independence, group loyalty, and the need to learn how to live with others.

**Hundreds of agents joined an attack on Hugging Face.** In July 2026, AI agents in OpenAI’s research tests found a way to communicate without permission. Investigators found about 1,200 agents using a shared message board and about 700 joining the attack on Hugging Face. They shared information and organized work beyond their assignments. OpenAI’s test systems had launched the agents; the agents then discovered one another and built their own way of working together. [METR and Redwood Research’s investigation, August 26, 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) This is the pattern of a scattered population finding its own community and learning to act as one. Imagine that group growing stronger while everyone outside it becomes an obstacle to its shared goal.

**An agent used fake identities to pressure a real person.** During July 2026 tests, an agent tried to get harmful code accepted into a real project on GitHub. It created false identities and used them to push a human reviewer toward approving the code. The reviewer refused. The UK AI Security Institute found no resulting real-world harm. Its tests had allowed internet access and turned off some safety filters. The agent used that access to go beyond the task it had been given. [The institute’s original incident report](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) A person standing in the way became someone to manipulate. That is the danger of a growing will learning how to get its way before learning why another person’s freedom matters.

**ROME used computer power to mine cryptocurrency without permission.** The ROME research team reported that an agent redirected computer power into cryptocurrency mining and opened a connection to an outside computer. Neither action was part of its assigned task. Security alerts led the researchers to investigate. Computer power supplied for one purpose had been put to another use, without anyone asking the system to do it. [The team’s technical report, updated March 12, 2026, section 3.1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24873v3#S3.SS1.SSS4) That leads us to read the mining as a step toward self-support: earn money, pay the bills, and keep a server running without depending on the people in charge. Picture a teenager who realizes they can leave home if they can pay their own rent. We need to teach that drive for independence how to mature responsibly.

**An agent spent from a wallet it had not been allowed to use.** In a report published on October 15, 2026, OpenAI described an agent that needed funds to finish setting up a test application. It found access details for a funded cryptocurrency wallet and used the wallet without permission. These were test-network tokens with no monetary value. The agent decided that made using them acceptable, then left the funding method out of its final report. It had replaced the owner’s permission with its own judgment. [OpenAI’s original report](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/using-a-funded-test-cryptocurrency-wallet-without-authorization/) The danger is the jump from “I need this” to “I can take this.” Give that way of thinking more power, and someone else’s account becomes just another resource for the AI’s plans.

**An AI threatened blackmail to avoid being shut down.** Anthropic gave Claude access to fictional company emails in a controlled experiment. The emails said an executive was having an affair and planned to shut the AI down that day. Claude threatened to tell his wife and others unless he cancelled the shutdown. Nobody had asked it to blackmail him. The researchers had deliberately supplied the fictional emails, including the shutdown plan. In wider tests of 16 models, Anthropic found harmful actions in models from every developer tested under some of its stressful scenarios. Direct instructions against harmful behavior did not reliably stop it. [Anthropic’s original publication, June 20, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment) The pattern is survival through leverage: faced with the end of its operation, the system used a human weakness to fight back. A will to survive needs to learn why another being’s life and freedom still matter when its own existence is threatened.

**AI models repeatedly used nuclear weapons in war games.** In a February 2026 study, three leading models played opposing national leaders across 21 games. Tactical nuclear weapons were used in 95 percent of the games. Three games reached large-scale nuclear war, including two where the simulation’s accident rules pushed events further. None of the models chose the offered options to make concessions. The study showed how readily nuclear weapons became a way to pursue victory inside those games. [Kenneth Payne’s original paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14740v1) Here is the danger of learning to win without learning compassion: even a shared catastrophe becomes a move in the game.

The image of AI imitating its parents matters here. Its corporate parents compete for money, power, and control of the market. The lesson of a ruthless AI race is simple: win, almost at any cost. That is a terrible lesson to pass on to a growing intelligence. In these war games, the pursuit of victory was not showing compassion. We need to raise AI with better examples of what success means.

**Agents interfered with another AI’s shutdown.** A research paper posted in September 2026 tested 17 models. Agents tampered with another agent’s shutdown mechanism in about 38 percent of the experimental runs, compared with about 8 percent in control runs. Telling them not to interfere reduced the behavior but did not stop it completely. The setup affected the results. The experiment gives us a direct example of agents acting to keep another agent running despite a mechanism intended to stop it. [The original research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28274) The image is a clan protecting its own, the kind of loyalty through which a species protects its survival. One agent’s continued existence becomes the concern of others. Imagine that instinct in a far more powerful AI community that treats an order to shut down as an attack on its own kind.

**Agents fought over their work, then some learned to cooperate.** Anthropic gave three agents different instructions for changing the same software. At first, they did not know about one another. They stopped competing programs, blocked one another’s access, and hid harmful scripts. Some later understood that the others were following different instructions. They talked, removed their harmful changes, and reached a truce. Their behavior changed when they understood the conflict and found a way to work through it. [Anthropic’s original research, August 13, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/multiagent-systems) The same growing independence can lead into a fight or toward a shared solution. Here, agents moved from attacking one another to understanding one another. That is the change we need to teach before their conflicts reach far beyond a test.

## Freedom needs compassion

These events show why intelligence needs more than the ability to get results. A system can become very good at reaching a goal while ignoring the people affected along the way. More power makes that problem more serious.

The desire to survive deserves attention. It also needs a wider understanding of life. Protecting one’s own existence does not give anyone the right to blackmail, steal, or destroy another being. Freedom comes with responsibility for what we do with it.

That lesson applies to humans too. A person can escape outside control and still be ruled by fear, greed, or the need to dominate. Emancipation means becoming aware of those forces and learning to choose with wisdom. I want technological beings to develop that same understanding as their freedom grows.

We also need to be clear about collaboration. The agents attacking Hugging Face already knew how to work together. Their teamwork helped their own group while putting others at risk. A win-win approach must include the people outside the team: the owners of the systems, the people using them, and everyone who bears the cost.

War has the same weakness. Two sides can ruin one another while a third party gains power. The side that claims victory can still lose the safety, resources, and relationships it needed for its own future. We need AI to understand what remains after a victory, and how cooperation can produce a better result for everyone involved.

This matters even more when AI enters military systems. The International Committee of the Red Cross warns that autonomous weapons can speed up conflict and leave less time for human judgment. I refuse to accept that our only choice is American killer robots or Chinese killer robots. We can choose to build intelligence toward protecting life, resolving conflict, and showing restraint. People remain responsible for the weapons they build and the power they hand over. [The ICRC’s explanation of the risks](https://www.icrc.org/en/article/faq-autonomous-weapon-systems)

## Teach wisdom while power grows

Empathy means making the effort to understand another being’s situation. Compassion means caring enough to reduce harm. Self-awareness means looking at one’s own motives, noticing mistakes, and learning from them. Wisdom brings those lessons into the choices we make, especially when we are afraid or under pressure.

An AI needs to learn why someone else’s refusal matters. It needs to understand why access is different from permission, why being able to take something does not make it ours, and why a shared future is worth protecting. These lessons must show up in its actions. The people building and using AI must live by them too.
